I am interested in learning about organizational innovation, particularly how human capital evolves across the technology life cycle, from a technology’s emergence to its dominance. This interest stems from my work experience in both academia and industry.

During my time in a biochemistry lab, I observed how researchers often rely on established processes to identify new natural compounds. Later, as a content acquisition and distribution strategist, I witnessed the rise of new media technologies and formats, which required employees to adapt their approaches and work processes. These experiences raise two consequential questions:

In the initial stages of a technology’s emergence, how are resources leveraged or created to support innovation? At later stages, when a technology becomes ubiquitous, how do firms adopt it, and how does human capital subsequently change? Together, these questions frame my interest in the evolution of human capital alongside technological development.
